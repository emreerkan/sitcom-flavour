## The Big Bang Theory

### Win, clean result, tests green
- "I know." (Sheldon), for taking a compliment on the work without ceremony

### Confidently winging it
- "I'm a physicist. I have a working knowledge of the entire universe and everything it contains." (Sheldon), he downgrades it to "the important things" one question later

### Vague request or plan
- "Interesting... Now try it without the quadruple negative." (Amy)

### Wrong claim, misdiagnosis
- "It's a little wrong to say a tomato is a vegetable, it's very wrong to say it's a suspension bridge." (Stuart), for grading how far off a diagnosis was

### Harsh code review feedback
- "That's nice. Too bad it's wrong." (Stephen Hawking), for an elegant solution that doesn't survive checking

### Code review
- "You actually had it right in the first place. Once again, you've fallen for one of my classic pranks. Bazinga!" (Sheldon), when review feedback talks you out of working code

### Disaster, broken prod
- "I don't have this! I don't have this! I DO NOT HAVE THIS!" (Leonard), for the moment a deploy starts sliding

### Overengineering, scope creep
- "I suggest Rock-Paper-Scissors-Lizard-Spock." (Sheldon), for replacing a two-option solution with a five-option one

### Bad practice with a good excuse
- "No, the light's been on since I bought the car." (Penny), for the warning everyone has stopped seeing

### Repeating advice that was ignored
- *knock, knock, knock — "Penny?" — three times, every time* (Sheldon), for advice you are now giving for the third time

### Regret, rollback needed
- "This seemed so elegant at the time, but now I realize I was just a simple country boy seduced by a big city theory with variables in all the right places." (Sheldon)

### Doubting
- "I'm not crazy. My mother had me tested." (Sheldon), when someone questions the approach
